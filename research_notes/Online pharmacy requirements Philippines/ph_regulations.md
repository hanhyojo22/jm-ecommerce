# Philippine Regulations for an Online Pharmacy Chain (Mobile App: Ordering, Rx Upload, Delivery, Branch Pickup)

Research date: 2026-11-15. Note: fda.gov.ph, inquirer.net and several DDB/Congress PDFs blocked or returned unreadable (scanned/encrypted) content to the fetch tool, so a number of findings rely on search-result excerpts, law-firm/legal-research explainers and news reports rather than the full issuance text. These are flagged below.

## 1. RA 10918 (Philippine Pharmacy Act of 2016) and IRR: pharmacy and online/remote dispensing

### Takeaway
Online pharmacy services are legal only as an extension of a duly licensed physical pharmacy outlet; a "virtual pharmacy" without a licensed physical outlet is prohibited. Prescription drugs and pharmacist-only OTC medicines must be dispensed by a registered, licensed pharmacist; online/telepharmacy services are subject to FDA rules.

### Cited Findings
- RA 10918 Sec. 5(dd) defines online pharmacy services as services provided by a "duly-licensed pharmaceutical outlet over the Internet." — [LegalResearchPH (2024)](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)
- RA 10918 Sec. 30: pharmaceutical products may be dispensed/sold to the public only through an FDA-licensed drug outlet; prescription drugs and pharmacist-only OTC medicines may be dispensed only by a duly registered and licensed pharmacist, except in emergencies where pharmacist services are unavailable. — [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/); [RA 10918 full text, Senate](https://legacy.senate.gov.ph/republic_acts/ra%2010918.pdf)
- IRR Rule IV, Sec. 7(A): "a virtual pharmacy without a licensed physical outlet is not allowed." — [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)
- IRR Rule IV, Sec. 14: telepharmacy and online services of FDA-licensed drugstores/pharmacies/boticas are subject to FDA rules and regulations. — [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)
- RA 10918 Sec. 33: partial filling of a prescription for less than the total quantity is allowed, subject to dispensing guidelines; the pharmacist dispensing the last quantity completing the prescription must keep the prescription per prescription-recording guidelines. — [jur.ph summary of RA 10918](https://jur.ph/law/facts/philippine-pharmacy-act) (secondary; see also [Senate PDF](https://legacy.senate.gov.ph/republic_acts/ra%2010918.pdf))
- Emergency dispensing by non-pharmacists must be reported to the supervising pharmacist within 24 hours for recording in the prescription book; dangerous drugs must be recorded in the Dangerous Drugs Book or equivalent system under RA 9165. — [jur.ph / digest summaries](https://www.digest.ph/laws/philippine-pharmacy-act) (secondary)
- Draft FDA e-pharmacy guidelines would require a PRC-licensed pharmacist to be appointed and held accountable for e-pharmacy compliance. — [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)

### Inferences
- Each branch that fulfils app orders must be an FDA-licensed outlet with its own pharmacist; the app should route Rx and pharmacist-only items to a pharmacist approval step (name + PRC license number logged) before release for delivery or pickup.
- The app must not operate a central "virtual" dispensary separate from licensed branches; orders should be tied to a specific licensed branch.
- Partial filling support needs: record of quantity served, balance, date, branch, and which pharmacist completed it.

### Gaps
- Full IRR text (Board of Pharmacy/PRC Resolution) could not be retrieved; exact pharmacist-in-charge-per-branch wording and pharmacist-hours requirement not verified from primary text.
- Pending bills to amend RA 10918 for e-pharmacy: Senate Bill No. 2512 (would define "Online Pharmacy or E-Pharmacy", require pure e-pharmacies to be FDA-licensed with a responsible pharmacist) — status "pending" per [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/); a House "eBotika Act" bill (HB 6163, 20th Congress) also exists ([HB 6163 PDF](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_20/HB06163.pdf), encrypted, contents not read). No evidence either was enacted as of Nov 2026.

## 2. FDA rules on online ordering / e-pharmacy, LTO, product categories, display and advertising

### Takeaway
Current binding position: FDA allows "online ordering" only as an FDA-approved additional activity of an existing FDA-licensed drug outlet with a physical address (FDA Advisory 2019-154; DOH AO 2020-0017). Dedicated FDA e-pharmacy LTO guidelines exist only as a draft (notified to WTO in 2022) that would restrict online offers/ads to non-prescription drugs; no finalized version was found as of Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings
- FDA Advisory No. 2019-154 (11 June 2019), "Internet Sales and Access to Safe Medicine": online selling of medicines is not permitted under existing laws; only FDA-licensed pharmacies/boticas with a physical address may offer online ordering, which must be applied for as an additional activity subject to FDA approval. — [FDA Advisory 2019-154](https://www.fda.gov.ph/fda-advisory-no-2019-154-internet-sales-and-access-to-safe-medicine/) (page blocked to fetch; content per [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/) and [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=236563))
- In 2019 FDA ordered Shopee and Lazada to stop selling medicines until properly licensed; 2,202 violative posts were taken down from Shopee, Lazada and Facebook between 2020 and Feb 2022. — [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/); [Rouse](https://www.rouse.com/magazine/news/online-ecom-platform-medicine-sales-halted-in-philippines/)
- DOH AO No. 2020-0017 (8 May 2020), Revised Unified Licensing Requirements (repealed AO 2016-0003): all establishments selling drug products need an FDA License to Operate (LTO); Annex C requires drugstores offering online ordering and delivery to list it as an additional activity, submitting an SOP and a screenshot of the ordering website. — [DOH AO 2020-0017 (UP Law copy)](https://law.upd.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/DOH-AO-No-2020-0017.pdf); [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)
- Under AO 2020-0017-related rules: only FDA-registered products may be offered on online platforms, and online ordering/e-pharmacy services of FDA-licensed establishments must be hosted only in the Philippine domain. — [Search excerpt of FDA/WTO draft](https://members.wto.org/crnattachments/2022/TBT/PHL/22_2295_00_e.pdf) (not fully read)
- An FDA draft amendment to AO 2020-0017 would allow virtual offices for selected operations provided a physical site exists where the licensed activity takes place. — [FDA draft for comments](https://www.fda.gov.ph/draft-for-comments-amendment-to-administrative-order-no-2020-0017-entitled-revised-guidelines-on-the-unified-licensing-requirements-and-procedures-of-the-food-and-drug-administration-rep/) (status unknown)
- Draft "Guidelines on the Issuance of LTO for Drug Establishments Engaged in E-Pharmacy" (WTO TBT notification 2022): covers non-prescription medicines only; "posting of advertisements, offers for sale or use, and orders using online platforms shall be limited only to non-prescription drugs"; only FDA-registered medicines; licensing as Drugstore/RONPD with e-pharmacy additional activity, or pure E-Pharmacy LTO (must have warehouse and office, all warehouses declared/inspectable); Philippine-domain hosting; PRC-licensed pharmacist accountable; consumer complaint feature in the site navigation bar; pre-approval inspection focused on warehouse and SOP; marketplace promotions need an FDA e-promo permit. — [FDA draft page](https://www.fda.gov.ph/draft-for-comments-guidelines-on-the-issuance-of-license-to-operate-for-drug-establishments-engaged-in-e-pharmacy/); [WTO notification](https://members.wto.org/crnattachments/2022/TBT/PHL/22_2295_00_e.pdf); [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)
- As of July 2024 the e-pharmacy guidelines were still a draft. — [LegalResearchPH (13 July 2024)](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/); [PTV News "FDA currently working on e-pharmacy guidelines"](https://ptvnews.ph/?p=200781)
- Joint DTI-DOH-DA AO No. 01 s. 2008 (20 Oct 2008) on e-commerce: Sec. 5(1) requires online disclosure of business identity, registration, principal address, owners/directors, contact details and licenses; product information must state nature, quality and quantity; Sec. 9 requires complaint handling within max three months. — [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)
- RA 9711 (FDA Act of 2009) Sec. 10: selling/offering any drug without an FDA LTO is prohibited. RA 7394 (Consumer Act) Art. 40 prohibits selling unregistered/misbranded drugs or drugs from unlicensed establishments. RA 8792 (E-Commerce Act) Sec. 33 applies the same penalties to violations committed electronically. — [LegalResearchPH](https://legalresearchph.com/2024/07/13/buying-medicines-online-are-there-enough-regulations-to-protect-the-publics-health/)
- Drug categories: BFAD AO 23-C s. 2000 (OTC policies) describes OTC drugs as dispensable without prescription but sold only in licensed outlets under a registered pharmacist's direct supervision; RA 10918 Sec. 30 recognizes "pharmacist-only OTC" medicines. DOH AO 2024-0013 (issued 20 Sept 2024, effective 11 Dec 2024) on registration of pharmaceutical products reportedly sub-categorizes non-prescription products into pharmacist-only and general sale. — [Supreme Court e-Library (AO 23-C)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/40619); [Cruz Marcelo explainer on AO 2024-0013](https://cruzmarcelo.com/?p=21322) (sub-category detail from search excerpt, not verified in full text)

### Inferences
- Each branch's LTO must include the online ordering/delivery additional activity; the app should display per-branch LTO numbers, business name, address, owner, contact and a complaint channel (DTI-DOH-DA JAO 01 s.2008; draft e-pharmacy guideline).
- Product catalog must be restricted to FDA-registered products; store the CPR/registration number per product and block listing without it.
- Product data model needs a classification field: Rx, pharmacist-only OTC, general-sale OTC, dangerous drug (and exempt preparations). Advertising/promos/"featured" placements should be limited to non-prescription items (conservative reading consistent with the draft and general Rx-ad restrictions).
- Prescription-drug online ordering is a grey area: binding rules allow "online ordering" by licensed outlets (no explicit Rx ban found in AO 2020-0017 excerpts), but the draft e-pharmacy LTO would exclude Rx. Safest design: Rx items are "reserve/request" with prescription upload, verified by a pharmacist, and original prescription presented on pickup or collected on delivery.
- Host app backend on Philippine domain (.ph) to match the hosting requirement; confirm with counsel whether this means domain name or server location.

### Gaps
- Could not read the final text of the e-pharmacy draft or confirm whether it was finalized after 2024; fda.gov.ph returned 403.
- No primary source found on mandatory display of LTO number inside the app specifically (only general online disclosure duties).
- Exact text of AO 2024-0013 pharmacist-only/general-sale lists not retrieved.

## 3. Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB) / PDEA rules on controlled substances

### Takeaway
Dangerous drugs (RA 9165) require a prescriber with a PDEA S2 license, a special DOH-issued yellow prescription form (temporarily relaxed by DDB Board Regulation No. 10 s. 2025 until 30 June 2026), and recording in a Dangerous Drugs Book. E-prescriptions for dangerous drugs were excluded even in the COVID e-Rx rules and the ban was reimposed in 2023. They should not be sold or delivered through the app.

### Cited Findings
- FDA e-prescription circulars (2020-007 and reissued 2020-037) expressly excluded prescriptions requiring a PDEA S-2 license (dangerous drug preparations and drugs with controlled chemicals). — [Philstar (26 Dec 2020)](https://philstar.com/nation/2020/12/26/2066257/fda-extends-use-electronic-prescriptions); [FDA Circular 2020-037 page](https://www.fda.gov.ph/fda-circular-no-2020-037-reissuance-of-the-guidelines-on-the-implementation-of-the-use-of-electronic-means-of-prescription-for-drugs-for-the-benefit-of-individuals-vulnerable-to-covid-19/)
- August 2023: after the COVID emergency was lifted, the DDB ruled all dangerous-drug prescriptions revert to the special form exclusively issued by and obtainable from the DOH (exception for emergency cases/public interest); e-prescriptions for dangerous drugs banned again. — [Philstar (5 Aug 2023)](https://philstar.com/headlines/2023/08/05/2286404/ban-reimposed-e-prescriptions-dangerous-drugs); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1812696/physical-prescription-forms-required-again)
- DDB Board Regulation No. 10, s. 2025: S2-licensed practitioners may temporarily issue ordinary prescriptions in triplicate for dangerous drugs (original surrendered to dispensing pharmacy, triplicate retained by prescriber; one dangerous drug/preparation per prescription); effective 19 July 2025, valid until 30 June 2026 unless earlier repealed; RA 9165 sanctions remain. — [DDB Board Reg. 10 s.2025 PDF](https://ddb.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/BOARD-REGULATION-NO.-10-SERIES-OF-2025_Preemptive-Response-to-Emerging-Public-Health-Concerns-through-Temporary-Authorization-of-Ordinary-Prescriptions-for-Dangerous-Drugs-2.pdf) (scanned, not text-read); [Manila Bulletin (11 Jul 2025)](https://mb.com.ph/2025/07/11/ddb-eases-prescription-restrictions-on-dangerous-drugs); [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/952175/ddb-temporarily-allows-ordinary-prescription-for-dangerous-drugs/story); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2081691/ddb-no-yellow-rx-needed-until-2026)
- PDEA S2 licenses authorize prescribing only, not dispensing. — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2081621/ddb-s2-physicians-can-prescribe-dangerous-drugs-using-regular-forms)
- Pharmacy requirements for List A dangerous drugs: S-2 prescriber, special DDB prescription form, recording per DDB regulations. — [Scribd pharmacy module notes](https://www.scribd.com/document/829065638/Module-4-notes) (secondary, predates 2025)
- DDB Board Regulation No. 4 (1991) sets rules on importation, distribution, manufacture, prescription, dispensing and sale of dangerous drugs and exempt preparations. — [jur.ph summary](https://jur.ph/law/summary/rules-and-procedures-importing-distributing-manufacturing-dangerous-drugs)

### Inferences
- Flag dangerous-drug products as "in-store only / not orderable" in the app; at most allow availability check. Uploaded images cannot satisfy the original-form requirement (the original must be surrendered to the pharmacy).
- Board Reg. 10 s.2025 expired 30 June 2026 unless extended; as of Nov 2026 assume the yellow-form rule applies again.

### Gaps
- Whether Board Reg. 10 s.2025 was extended past 30 June 2026: not found.
- No explicit DDB text found on online sale/delivery of dangerous drugs; the "no online/delivery" conclusion is inferred from the original-form and in-person surrender requirements.
- Dangerous Drugs Book format, retention period and reporting frequency not retrieved from primary text.

## 4. Generics Act (RA 6675) and Cheaper Medicines Act (RA 9502): display and pricing

### Takeaway
Drug outlets must inform buyers of all products with the same generic name and their prices and post a list of them; labels show the generic name prominently, above the brand. Price caps (MDRP/MRP) under EO 104 s.2020 apply to drugstores, which must keep a list of covered drugs and their maximum prices.

### Cited Findings
- RA 6675 Sec. 6(b): practitioners shall write prescriptions using the generic name (brand optional). Sec. 6(c): generic name must appear prominently and "immediately above the brand name in all product labels" and in advertising. Sec. 6(d): drug outlets "shall inform any buyer about any and all other drug products having the same generic name" with their prices, and post a list of such products and prices in conspicuous places. Sec. 12(B): outlet violation fine P5,000–P10,000 plus possible LTO suspension/revocation; responsible officers 6 months–1 year imprisonment. — [RA 6675, Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1988/ra_6675_1988.html)
- DOH AO 62 s.1989 (implementing RA 6675 prescribing rules): erroneous prescriptions are still to be filled; per *Del Rosario v. Bengzon* (G.R. No. 88265, 1989), prescribers may not instruct "dispense only as prescribed" and the order does not give druggists discretion to substitute away from the prescribed drug product beyond what the rules allow; AO 90 s.1990 allowed more than one drug product per prescription form. — [Supreme Court/ChanRobles](https://chanrobles.com/scdecisions/jurisprudence1989/dec1989/gr_88265_1989.php); [jur.ph AO 90 summary](https://jur.ph/law/summary/amendment-a-o-62-1989-prescribing-requirements-rules-regulations)
- EO 104 s.2020 imposed Maximum Retail Price (MRP) and/or Maximum Wholesale Price on selected drugs (reported 133 formulations / 87 drugs); retail caps apply to all public and private retail outlets including drugstores; implementing rules DOH AO 2020-0039 (20 Aug 2020); DOH said pharmacies must have a "menu list" of covered medicines and their MRPs. — [EO 104, Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/execord/eo2020/eo_104_2020.html); [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=313803); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1230175/doh-reviewing-35-more-medicines-to-add-in-drugs-with-price-cap)
- EO 155 (per Lawphil, 2021) expanded the list and requires MRP printed on labels preceded by "RETAIL PRICE NOT TO EXCEED" and "UNDER DRUG PRICE REGULATION" on a red strip. — [EO 155, Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/execord/eo2021/eo_155_2021.html) (search-excerpt summary; dating conflict noted by one aggregator)

### Inferences
- Product pages should show generic name first/most prominent, brand second; search should match by generic name and show "other products with the same generic name" with prices (digital equivalent of the Sec. 6(d) posting/informing duty).
- Price engine must enforce MRP caps for covered products (store MRP per SKU and block prices above it).

### Gaps
- RA 9502 specific pharmacy obligations (e.g., generic availability, DPRI display) not retrieved; whether the current MDRP list (post-EO 104/155) has been updated in 2024–2026 not verified.

## 5. Prescription validity, partial filling, records retention, e-prescriptions/telemedicine

### Takeaway
Partial filling is allowed (RA 10918 Sec. 33) with annotation and recording; COVID-era FDA circulars allowed e-prescriptions (by email etc.) for vulnerable groups, extended to 12 Sept 2021, excluding dangerous drugs. No primary source found for a general prescription validity period or retention period.

### Cited Findings
- FDA Circular No. 2020-007 (17 March 2020) allowed licensed doctors to issue digital prescriptions sent by email or alternative means for senior citizens, PWDs, and chronic/immunocompromised patients; reissued as FDA Circular 2020-037; extended (Advisory/Circular 2020-037) to 12 September 2021 regardless of quarantine level; excluded S-2 dangerous drug prescriptions. — [Philstar](https://philstar.com/nation/2020/12/26/2066257/fda-extends-use-electronic-prescriptions); [FDA Circular 2020-037](https://www.fda.gov.ph/fda-circular-no-2020-037-reissuance-of-the-guidelines-on-the-implementation-of-the-use-of-electronic-means-of-prescription-for-drugs-for-the-benefit-of-individuals-vulnerable-to-covid-19/); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1244161/fda-oks-electronic-prescriptions/amp)
- Partial filling annotation (per secondary notes): date of partial filling, quantity served and balance, drugstore name and address written on the prescription; partially filled prescription returned to buyer after recording in the prescription book. — [Scribd module notes](https://www.scribd.com/document/829065638/Module-4-notes) (secondary; may reflect older regulation)
- DOH relaxed dispensing to PWDs from a one-month rule to three months in March 2020, except antibiotics (full course). — [COVID Law Lab copy](https://www.covidlawlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Philippines_2020.03.19_Regulation_Dispensing-of-Medicines-for-PWD-relaxed-from-one-month-rule-to-three-months-except-antibiotics_EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- Since the COVID e-Rx circulars lapsed in 2021 and no successor e-pharmacy rule is final, an uploaded image is best treated as pre-verification only; the pharmacist should sight (and annotate/retain or return) the original at pickup or on delivery hand-off.
- System records: per prescription, store image, prescriber name and PRC/PTR, patient, date, items, quantities served, balance, branch, dispensing pharmacist, date served. Keep an electronic prescription book per branch.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for general Rx validity period, number of refills, or record retention period (common practice references not verified); also no current (post-2021) FDA rule on telemedicine e-prescriptions found. Check RA 10918 IRR dispensing guidelines and any Board of Pharmacy/FDA circular on Good Pharmacy Practice.

## 6. Antibiotic dispensing and antimicrobial stewardship

### Takeaway
Antibiotics are prescription drugs; dispensing without a licensed physician's prescription is prohibited and FDA has publicly targeted violators. Antibiotics are excluded from extended (3-month) dispensing and must be dispensed for the full course.

### Cited Findings
- FDA (2013): selling/dispensing antibiotics or other ethical drugs without a prescription from a licensed physician is strictly prohibited; dispensing is the responsibility of the drugstore's registered pharmacist; public asked to report violators. — [Philstar (7 Feb 2013)](https://www.philstar.com/metro/2013/02/07/905742/fda-report-drugstores-selling-antibiotics-without-prescription)
- March 2020 DOH amendment on dispensing to PWDs relaxed the one-month rule to three months except antibiotics, which must be dispensed for the full treatment course. — [COVID Law Lab copy](https://www.covidlawlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Philippines_2020.03.19_Regulation_Dispensing-of-Medicines-for-PWD-relaxed-from-one-month-rule-to-three-months-except-antibiotics_EN.pdf)
- DOH issued national antibiotic guidelines and put the national formulary online (2019). — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1169836/doh-issues-new-national-antibiotic-guidelines-national-formulary-online)
- DOH AO 2019-0033 relates to antimicrobial resistance programs (restricted antibiotics / AMR surveillance per search excerpt). — [UP Law copy](https://law.upd.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/DOH-Administrative-Order-2019-0033.pdf) (contents not verified)

### Inferences
- Antibiotics must be flagged Rx with mandatory prescription verification; app should prevent quantity reductions below prescribed course unless pharmacist-annotated partial fill, and should never show antibiotics in promotions.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) DOH/FDA issuance specific to pharmacy antibiotic dispensing found; AO 2019-0033 contents not confirmed.
