# Scanning cannot verify Philippine discount entitlement

No OCR pipeline can verify a Philippine senior citizen or PWD ID, because the entitlement lives in LGU-issued cards with no national template, no checksum, no MRZ and, for seniors, no registry at all; the Department of Justice held in October 2026 that a PWD discount must be granted on presentation, which rules out gating a sale on any automated check. What software can do is narrower and cheaper: read the card well enough to save typing, verify a PhilID's signed QR for identity and age, check a Unified PWD ID against `verify.alaga.ph` where coverage exists, catch the same ID or portrait reused across accounts, and record a staff confirmation at handover. For JMMart the defensible build is a credential vault plus on-device capture gating plus post-hoc audit — roughly **₱15,000–24,000 for the vault, and a further ₱24,000–36,000 if OCR assist proves worthwhile** — not a **$0.33–$1.89-per-check eKYC contract** that verifies the buyer rather than the beneficiary and still cannot see the LGU record that decides eligibility. The cheapest controls are also the ones that survive a privacy audit and a 70-year-old customer holding a laminated card in a dim pharmacy queue.

## The discount is an entitlement question, not a document question

Philippine law triggers the 20% medicine discount on **presentation**, not on prior approval. RA 9994 §4 accepts an OSCA-issued ID honoured nationwide, a Philippine passport, or other documents establishing citizenship and age ([RA 9994](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_9994_2010.html)); the IRR requires a "**valid and original**" card at the point of sale ([RA 9994 and IRR](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_9994_2010.html)). For delivery orders, IRR Art. 7 §3(g) is explicit: the card number is given when ordering and the **card must be presented again on delivery**. RA 10754 mirrors the structure for PWDs, with an LGU-issued ID, a passport, or an NCWDP transport ID as proof ([RA 10754](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2016/ra_10754_2016.html)), and the FDA confirms that seniors **or their representatives** may claim the discount on any of the listed IDs ([FDA Advisory 2024-0429](https://fda.gov.ph/fda-advisory-no-2024-0429-clarification-on-the-requirements-for-senior-citizens-to-avail-20-discount-on-medicines/)).

The DOJ's October 2026 opinion and the IRR are not in conflict, and reading them as a conflict produces the wrong product. The DOJ held that "**prior verification of the validity of a PWD ID is not allowed**… you have to extend the benefit already upon presentation," while allowing subsequent validation ([Newswatch Plus, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.newswatchplus.ph/2026/10/09/ncda-flags-nearly-6m-fake-pwd-ids)). The IRR requires the card again at handover. Together they say the sale is never gated and the check moves to the counter and to after-the-fact audit. Any "pending verification" state must therefore attach to the **account**, never to the order in flight; a customer who has not yet been verified still checks out at the discounted price and is checked at handover.

The compliance artifact is a sales record, not a document collection. BIR RR 7-2010 §7(6), as amended by RR 11-2015, requires the senior's name and identification document alongside gross sales, discount, date and invoice number ([BIR RR 7-2010](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/55830); [BIR digest RR 11-2015](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/Digest%20RR%2011-2015.pdf)); RR 5-2017 §5.9 requires the PWD's name and ID number, and disallows the deduction if either is missing ([BIR RR 5-2017](https://ncda.gov.ph/disability-laws/implementing-rules-and-regulations-irr/revenue-regulations-no-5-2017-rules-and-regulations-implementing-republic-act-no-10754)). A photocopy is required only when a benefactor claims a dependent exemption, not when a pharmacy grants the discount. The tax rule needs a **number and a name**, which is the strongest argument for storing fields rather than images.

Identity is not eligibility, and this is the fact that decides the architecture. RA 11055 §14 states that "**proof of identity shall not necessarily be construed as proof of eligibility** to avail of certain benefits" ([RA 11055](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11055_2018.html)). PhilSys can prove that a person is who they say they are and that they are at least 60; it holds no OSCA registration and no disability status. The single mature national registry in the country answers a question adjacent to the one the discount asks.

JMMart already has the legally important part. Orders snapshot `discount_card_type`, an encrypted `discount_card_number`, `discount_card_name` and `discount_amount`; the fulfilling branch sees the name and full number for the handover comparison at `resources/views/seller/orders/show.blade.php:98`; `RemoveSeniorPwdDiscount` reverses the discount only on unpaid COD orders and tells staff to cancel a prepaid order instead (`app/Actions/RemoveSeniorPwdDiscount.php:32-38`). That asymmetry is precisely where a verified vault earns its keep: COD already has a physical check and a remedy, prepaid does not. A reusable credential is an **optimisation and a prepaid-risk control**, not a new compliance obligation. Liability splits the same way: RA 11967 makes the seller primarily liable and the marketplace subsidiarily liable ([RA 11967](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2023/ra_11967_2023.html)), which argues for the branch being the merchant of record for the discounted sale and the BIR invoice, with the platform supplying the tooling and the verification log.

Four decisions follow, and they are product decisions before they are technical ones. What "verified" is allowed to mean in the UI; whether the account holder must be the ID holder (the FDA's representative rule says no, so collect a patient name and relationship instead of blocking); whether an image is stored at all (default: no); and whether prepaid discounts above a threshold require a credential already confirmed at handover. None of these requires an OCR vendor.

## Two registries answer part of it; neither answers all of it

The PhilID is the only Philippine credential with a cryptographic check, and it is not an OCR problem. Its QR carries JSON — name, suffix, sex, date and place of birth, PhilSys Card Number, issuance date and best fingerprint, plus a portrait on the ePhilID — signed by PSA, with public keys published so relying parties can verify **offline and free** ([PhilSys Check Handbook v3.0](https://verify.philsys.gov.ph/api/handbook)). The check returns verified, deactivated, unreadable/tampered, or offline ([PSA, 1 Feb 2024](https://philsys.gov.ph/philsys-authentication-services-strengthen-transaction-security/)). PSA's device floor is a **720p camera with at least 8 MP stills**, enhancements disabled, good lighting ([PhilSys Check](https://philsys.gov.ph/philsys-check)). Card, paper ePhilID and Digital National ID are all valid and carry no expiry for citizens ([PhilSys FAQ](https://philsys.gov.ph/faq-frequently-asked-questions/)). The correct build is a QR reader that cross-checks the printed name and date of birth against the signed payload; OCR-ing a PhilID throws away the one signal on the card that a counterfeiter cannot reproduce.

The API channel is real but gated. NIDAS offers Tier 0 QR, Tier I "verified/not verified" plus registered full name, and Tier II eKYC with demographics and the registration portrait, at **99.5% monthly availability, 1,000 transactions/minute and 1–5 second responses**, free "at present" to onboarded relying parties ([Didit, PhilSys verification, 6 Oct 2026](https://didit.me/blog/philsys-verification/), summarising PSA's eVerify FAQ). Onboarding runs a Letter of Intent and business-process map, a 30-day temporary key, a security assessment, a **VAPT**, and a one- or three-year contract signed by both DPOs — 7–10 working days of review and weeks to months end to end ([same](https://didit.me/blog/philsys-verification/)). The PSA prohibits storing the PhilSys Number itself; a relying party keeps a backend **token** and only the fields its agreement authorises ([PhilSys FAQ](https://philsys.gov.ph/faq-frequently-asked-questions/)). Vendors cannot be the relying party on their own ([Didit](https://didit.me/blog/philsys-verification/)), and a for-profit pharmacy's eligibility is unresolved: the public FAQ speaks of NGOs in social protection while eVerify's terms of use describe "private entities" generally ([eVerify Terms of Use](https://everify.gov.ph/terms-of-use)).

The PWD side changed in 2026, and the change is real but partial. NCDA AO 001 s.2026 created the Unified PWD ID and the Unified Persons with Disabilities Identification System: a standard template with a unique alphanumeric number and a **QR code checked against a national database at `verify.alaga.ph`**, lifetime validity for permanent disabilities, five-year revalidation for non-apparent disabilities, and a three-year transition during which existing authentic cards stay valid ([NCDA AO 001 s.2026](https://ncda.gov.ph/2026/09/09/institutionalization-and-implementation-of-a-unified-persons-with-disabilities-identification-system-administrative-order-no-001-series-of-2026/); [Rappler, 6 Nov 2026](https://rappler.com/philippines/persons-with-disabilities-registration-unified-pwd-id-system)). As of October 2026, **about 1,100 of 1,642 LGUs were onboarded, with more than 190,000 registered in UPWDIS and 128,000 digital IDs issued** against 2.9 million registered PWDs ([Rappler](https://rappler.com/philippines/persons-with-disabilities-registration-unified-pwd-id-system)). PhilHealth's own October 2026 circular still uses the older PRPWD "**for now, and the UPWDIS once**… fully operational" ([PhilHealth TS-PC-2026-0017](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/circulars/2026/TS-PC-2026-0017_English.pdf)).

That resolves the apparent contradiction between "PWD verification just became digital" and "there is no queryable PWD registry". The endpoint exists and is authoritative for cards inside it; what does not exist is a documented API, published rate limits, terms of use, or coverage of the legacy cards that remain valid until roughly 2029. `verify.alaga.ph` is a JavaScript web app with no published integration contract, and only PDAO officers and LGU IT staff can write to the registry ([Rappler](https://rappler.com/philippines/persons-with-disabilities-registration-unified-pwd-id-system)). The platform can **read and record a result**; it cannot enrol a customer, and it cannot yet depend on programmatic access.

Senior IDs remain a local-government patchwork. RA 9994 mandates a free card valid nationwide but prescribes no template, no expiry, no QR and no registry; issuance is by each city or municipal OSCA ([RA 9994](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_9994_2010.html)). Naga City's ordinance describes a laminated paper card with a barcode, while Valenzuela added a holographic seal and a **VAL ID** validation portal in 2025 ([Naga Ordinance 2018-082](https://www2.naga.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/ord2018-082.pdf); [Valenzuela City, 20 May 2025](https://valenzuela.gov.ph/article/valenzuela-unveils-new-design-for-pwd-and-senior-citizen-id-cards-launches-val-id-validation-portal)). The digital National Senior Citizens ID in eGovPH is valid for the discount and had **over 1.3 million users by April 2026**, but NCSC documents no merchant verification endpoint and the credential can be screenshotted ([NCSC digital NSCID](https://www.ncsc.gov.ph/ncsid); [Abogado report on NCSC Advisory](https://abogado.com.ph/govt-reminds-public-establishments-to-accept-digital-senior-citizen-id)).

| Credential | Machine check available | Coverage | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| PhilID / ePhilID / Digital National ID | PSA-signed QR, free and offline; NIDAS Tier I–II by contract | All issued IDs | Identity and date of birth; **not** eligibility (RA 11055 §14) |
| Unified PWD ID (2026) | QR against `verify.alaga.ph` | ~1,100 of 1,642 LGUs, expanding to 2029 | PWD registration in UPWDIS |

| OSCA senior ID | None | None | Nothing beyond physical inspection |

| Passport, DL, voter's, UMID, postal, PRC | No general third-party API; PRC LERIS verifies the licence, not the holder | n/a | Presence of a document, not entitlement |

The vendor layer reflects the same gap. Azure's `prebuilt-idDocument` documents worldwide passports, US and Indian documents and an "Other" bucket, with **no Philippine entry** ([Azure ID document model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/id-document?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)); AWS `AnalyzeID` is documented for US passports and licences ([AWS Textract pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/pricing)); Google's only identity parser is US-only ([Google Document AI processors](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/processors-list)). Microblink lists more than a dozen Philippine classes — PhilID, UMID, PhilHealth, Postal, PRC, SSS, TIN, Voter, Driver's Licence, AEP, Alien ID, eID — and **no senior or PWD card** ([BlinkID supported documents](https://docs.microblink.com/blinkid/supported-documents)). Regula's February 2026 release added a **barcode parser** for the "Philippines Senior Citizen ID Card" ([Regula 9.2 release notes](https://docs.regulaforensics.com/develop/doc-reader-sdk/release-notes/9-2)), which is data extraction, not authentication: no public specification exists for what that barcode encodes or whether any LGU signs it, and a barcode that merely repeats the printed number is trivially reproducible.

## OCR reads cards without authenticating them

A production ID pipeline is staged: capture-quality gate, detect and crop, classify, OCR, extract by template or QR, validate, score, then accept, review or reject. The stages that decide real-world accuracy are the **first and the last**, not the recogniser. Google's own guidance is that each character should be at least 16×16 pixels, that there is no gain above 24×24, and that poor focus requires recapture ([ML Kit text recognition v2](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/v2/android)). That is the cheapest accuracy win available and it costs nothing: refuse the frame before uploading it.

Independent evidence puts classical OCR far below vendor headlines. On DocXPand-25k, 25,463 annotated identity-document fields, **Tesseract 5.3.3 reached 54.5% field accuracy**, with only 48.6% of MRZ fields read perfectly ([DocXPand-25k, arXiv 2407.20662](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.20662)). PaddleOCR's published PP-OCRv5 recognition is 86.79% on printed English but **58.06% on handwriting** for the server model, and 87.53% versus 49.44% for mobile ([PaddleOCR PP-OCRv5 docs](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/main/docs/version3.x/algorithm/PP-OCRv5/PP-OCRv5.en.md)). LayoutLMv3 falls from 96.6 F1 with ground-truth text to **80.5 F1 with a real OCR engine** ([See then Tell, arXiv 2409.19573](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19573v2)), and most large multimodal models score below 50/100 on OCRBench v2, with a 32-point gap between high- and low-frequency text ([OCRBench v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00321)). None of that evidence is Philippine: **no public benchmark exists for PhilID, OSCA or PWD cards**, which is the largest evidence gap in this decision.

Vendor numbers are upper bounds measured on curated images. Microblink claims "over 95%" extraction across 2,500+ documents ([Microblink](https://microblink.com/resources/blog/flutter-document-scanner)); Klippa claims "up to 99%" without publishing a test set ([Klippa](https://www.klippa.com/en/ocr/identity-documents/)); Accura's pricing page says 99.95% while its product page says 99.99% ([Accura pricing](https://accurascan.com/ocr-pricing)). Azure, AWS and Google publish coverage and confidence fields but **no per-field accuracy** for ID documents. The specialist SDKs' real asset is the document library, not the recogniser — and the library does not include the two card types that decide this business.

Authenticity from a single phone photo is narrower still. Template matching needs a reference design per document type, and LGU cards have none; the standard authenticity stack also includes UV/IR inks, optically variable behaviour, microprint and chip reading, none of which a commodity camera captures ([Regula Document Reader SDK](https://regulaforensics.com/products/document-reader-sdk)). Error-level analysis and JPEG-ghost detection are explicitly weak on recompressed images, screenshots and social-media downloads ([Photo & Video Forensics, 2026](https://maxintel.org/forensics-methods.html)), and the best published screen-recapture detector — a purpose-built 2024 model — still reports **16.78% equal error rate** ([CMA, CVPR 2024](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/cvpr/2024/530000p577/20hOYpKEb5u)). Holograms are view- and illumination-dependent by design; a 2015 controlled study had humans at 80% and the prototype at 73%, dropping to 50% on one of four holograms ([IEEE TVCG 2015](https://arbook.icg.tugraz.at/schmalstieg/Schmalstieg_291.pdf)), and the MIDV-Holo benchmark is built on video clips rather than stills ([MIDV-Holo, ICDAR 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-41682-8_30)). For a laminated LGU card, "authenticity" collapses to "looks like an ID and is not an obvious paste-up" — which a competent counterfeiter passes.

Biometrics answer a different question than the one being asked. Top submissions in NIST's FRTE 1:1 track report **0.16% FNMR on visa-to-border pairs and 3.75% on border-to-kiosk** ([NIST FRTE 1:1](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt11.html)), but the track does not test a selfie against a ten-year-old laminated portrait in pharmacy lighting. iBeta Level 2 letters report **0% APCER across 1,500 attacks** — with cooperative subjects, mid-level artefacts and 24 hours per attack instrument ([Shufti Pro Level 2 letter](https://www.ibeta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/250410-Shufti-Pro-PAD-Level-2-Confirmation-Letter.pdf)) — while camera injection defeats liveness outright: a consumer face-swap app plus OBS and a virtual-camera driver passed a bank's check on a non-rooted phone ([Biometric Update, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202512/iproov-shows-how-face-swapping-app-can-fool-liveness-detection-on-financial-apps)), and WEF catalogued **17 face-swap and 8 injection tools** in ten months ([WEF, 2026](https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Unmasking_Cybercrime_Strengthening_Digital_Identity_Verification_against_Deepfakes_2026.pdf)). NIST's passive PAD track is closed, so 2025–2026 claims rest on vendor letters. The population is also the least tested: iBeta's protocol requires subjects "willing and able to provide any and all biometric samples", which excludes tremor, facial palsy, cataracts and heavy glasses. And the structural mismatch is fatal for this use case: the person who uploads is often the daughter, while the person who must present the card is the senior. A face match on the buyer proves nothing about the beneficiary, and the FDA expressly permits representatives.

## The economics favour binding, not biometrics

Per-check pricing is not the constraint; it is a distraction from the engineering cost. At JMMart's plausible volumes, on-device ML Kit plus a 3% human-review rate costs about **₱30, ₱300 and ₱3,000 per month in review labour at 100, 1,000 and 10,000 verifications**; cloud OCR is ₱30, ₱300 and ₱3,783; a $0.33 eKYC check is **₱1,914, ₱19,140 and ₱191,400** ([Google Cloud Vision pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vision/pricing); [Didit pricing](https://didit.me/pricing/); planning assumptions: ₱58 = $1, 3% review, ₱10 per review). Five dev-days at ₱3,000 costs more than a decade of cloud OCR at 1,000 scans a month. Optimising the per-scan price is not the lever.

The break-even arithmetic is unforgiving. A ₱1,000 medicine basket yields a ₱200 discount; if **1% of 10,000 monthly claims are invalid and unrecoverable, leakage is ₱20,000 a month — roughly seven times the entire OCR and review budget**. To justify ₱191,400 a month of eKYC at that volume, the platform would need to prevent about **955 invalid ₱200 discounts a month, or 9.5% of all claims**. That is implausible for a discount that already has a physical handover check, and it is the wrong comparison anyway: the buy decision is about review-labour avoidance or fraud-loss reduction, never about OCR quality ([Verihubs, eKYC pricing Philippines](https://verihubs.com/ph/blog/ekyc-pricing-philippines)).

The fraud that actually occurs is cheap, physical and local. Valenzuela police seized **300+ fake government IDs** in September 2025, sold on social media for **₱40 to ₱1,000** each ([Inquirer, 10 Sep 2025](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2107461/couple-nabbed-for-mass-production-selling-fake-govt-ids-in-valenzuela)). One restaurant group rejected **726 of 4,212 PWD IDs (17.24%)** in a single audit, for wrong numbers, missing details, photocopies and unfamiliar formats — the last of which is also a false-reject category ([Rappler, 7 Dec 2024](https://www.rappler.com/newsbreak/in-depth/increase-fake-person-with-disability-ids-philippines/)). Senate estimates put illegitimate PWD ID use at **8.5 million against 1.89 million registered** in 2024 and **₱88.2 billion in lost revenue**, with 2.9 million registered by October 2026; these are panel estimates, not an audited count ([Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/newsbreak/in-depth/increase-fake-person-with-disability-ids-philippines/); [PIA, 4 Oct 2026](https://pia.gov.ph/news/luzon/calabarzon/unified-pwd-id-to-protect-benefits-curb-fake-ids/)). NCDA's own board resolution describes cards "purchased through online platforms or legitimately issued to non-eligible persons" ([NCDA Board Resolution 5, s.2025](https://ncda.gov.ph/a-resolution-creating-the-inter-agency-technical-working-group-on-the-development-of-the-unified-id-system-for-persons-with-disabilities-board-resolution-no-5-series-of-2025/)).

Saving an ID changes the threat model more than any OCR improvement can. A saved credential is a **bearer credential**: it can be reused, lent, sold or stolen, and the marketplace becomes its issuer without any ability to revoke it at the source. A per-order visual check becomes a one-time gate, so a single successful upload can be monetised indefinitely, and the cheapest attack stops being forgery and becomes **account sharing or resale**, which looks like normal repeat custom. The asset at risk is also larger than the discount: name, date of birth, ID number, address and photo are enough to order a fake ID or open synthetic accounts, and unlike a password an ID number cannot be rotated. Government ID data already circulates: ten agencies filed breach notifications in November 2026 with government IDs among the exposed categories ([Rappler, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.rappler.com/technology/npc-sounds-alarm-government-agencies-report-leaks-november-2026/)).

| Threat | Why it fits this flow | Primary control |
|---|---|---|
| Printed or template-generated LGU/PWD card | No uniform design; templates sold online for ₱40–₱1,000 | Handover inspection; UPWDIS or PhilID QR where available |
| Borrowed family card | Proxy purchase is lawful for seniors and widely reported | Patient name and relationship; handover; review, never auto-deny |
| Stolen real ID plus impostor | Government ID data already circulates | Duplicate and device signals; handover; step-up on anomalies |
| Edited photo or screenshot of a card | Gallery upload is the single biggest enabler | In-app camera, no gallery in the primary path, recapture signals |
| Account sharing or resale of a verified account | The saved ID is a bearer credential and looks like repeat custom | Device and address clustering; revalidation; revocation; step-up |
| Insider override at branch or support | Staff can mark a credential verified | Two-person rule for revocations; audit every reveal; QA sampling |

Base rates make review-queue design humbling. With 2% fraud prevalence, an 80%-recall detector and a 2% false-positive rate, **40 of 9,644 accepted applicants are fraudulent (0.41%), and only 45% of flagged cases are actually fraudulent**. A "2% false positive rate" produces a queue that is majority innocent, which is why escalation must be designed for recall and adjudicated by a rubric, not treated as a verdict. The same logic forbids hard weekly caps on medicine discounts: RA 9994's ₱125 weekly cap applies to basic necessities, not medicines ([RA 9994](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_9994_2010.html)), and chronic patients legitimately order often. The penalty asymmetry points the same way. Refusing to honour a valid senior card carries **2–6 years' imprisonment and a ₱50,000–₱100,000 fine on first offence**, while abusing the privilege carries **at least six months** ([RA 9994](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_9994_2010.html)). A false accept costs 20% of one basket; a false reject is a criminal complaint. Grant provisionally, verify at the counter, and recover afterwards.

The highest-return controls are free or nearly so. Exact ID-number hash across accounts, perceptual image hash on uploads, device and address clustering, velocity on discount value, and the branch's handover confirmation — which is a genuine positive label and should promote a credential from pending to verified after a few clean uses. None of these needs a vendor, a model or a biometric.

## Build a credential vault, gate capture, verify at handover

The repo is on **Laravel 13.33.0 and PHP 8.4.27**, not the Laravel 8.x / PHP 8.5 combination described in the brief. That matters only in that `encrypted` casts and `APP_PREVIOUS_KEYS` rotation behave as documented in 13.x, and `laravel/pennant` is not installed — so the pilot flag should be a config switch plus a per-user allowlist, not a new dependency. The architecture is one resolver, one vault and one confirmation loop. Flutter captures with the in-app camera (gallery disabled for the primary check, both sides in one session, a tilt prompt for glare, torch, and a permanently visible "type it instead"), runs ML Kit on-device for a capture gate and a field prefill, and posts **only the confirmed fields** — no image — on the default path. The ML Kit document scanner adds edge detection and cropping for about 300 KB but needs **1.7 GB of total device RAM** and throws `UNSUPPORTED` below it, so a `camera` + manual-crop fallback is required for hand-me-down phones ([ML Kit document scanner](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/doc-scanner/android)). Blade uses a file input with `capture="environment"` and the same server-side validation. Laravel resolves the credential in the existing `SeniorPwdDiscount::cardFrom()` seam, checks ownership and status server-side, prices quote and place through the identical path, returns a machine-readable `discount_credential.invalid` code on failure so the cart survives, and never lets the client assert "verified". The order's `discount_card_*` snapshot stays authoritative forever; the vault is a cache, and deleting it must not touch tax records.

Registry checks plug in behind a per-issuer adapter rather than a single integration. The PhilID adapter decodes the QR and verifies the PSA signature locally, storing the result, method and timestamp but never the PCN; NIDAS Tier I/II can replace it later behind the same interface if PSA accepts the platform as a relying party. The UPWDIS adapter is a human-assisted check against `verify.alaga.ph` today, recording the alphanumeric number and the verification result, and becomes an API call only when NCDA publishes terms. Everything else falls through to assisted entry and handover. The adapter shape is the point: coverage will change per LGU and per year, and the decision layer should not.

**Data model.** One table, one active row per user and type, encrypted number plus a blind index for duplicate detection.

| Column | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | ULID | Public identifier; never expose sequential IDs |
| `user_id` | FK users | Owner; cascade on delete |
| `type` | string(10) | `senior` or `pwd` |
| `id_number` | text, `encrypted` cast | Recoverable for BIR record and handover |
| `id_number_hash` | char(64) | HMAC-SHA256 blind index; duplicate detection |
| `id_number_last4` | char(4) | Support and display |
| `name_on_id` | text, `encrypted` | May legitimately differ from the account holder |
| `name_normalized` | string(160) | Accent-folded, whitespace-collapsed; ranking only |
| `issuer` | string(120), null | OSCA, LGU or PDAO name |
| `expires_at` | date, null | Null means "no printed expiry", the normal case |
| `revalidate_at` | date | Operational freshness check, not a legal expiry |
| `status` | string(20) | `pending`, `verified`, `rejected`, `expired`, `revoked` |
| `verification_method` | string(30) | `manual_entry`, `ocr_confirmed`, `staff_handover`, `registry_qr`, `admin_review` |
| `verification_ref` | string(120), null | Audit-log ID or QR result hash; no PII |
| `confidence` | decimal(4,3), null | OCR confidence, never a fraud score |
| `reviewed_by`, `reviewed_at` | FK, timestamp | Admin decision |
| `last_used_at`, `use_count` | timestamp, integer | Velocity |
| `superseded_by_id` | FK self, null | Re-issue and name-change chain |

Add a partial unique index on `(user_id, type)` where the status is active, a unique index on `id_number_hash` **where status = 'verified'** (applying it to `pending` creates false collisions from typos and abandoned carts), and an index on `last_used_at`. If adding `spatie/laravel-ciphersweet` is not approved, a plain HMAC-SHA256 column with a separate pepper achieves the same exact-match property; a bare SHA-256 of a 12-digit number is brute-forceable in minutes and is not acceptable.

**Phased rollout.** The order matters: the vault is the product feature, OCR is a UX feature, review is a risk control.

| Phase | Scope | Effort | Cost at ₱3,000/day | Gate to proceed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Policy (holder vs representative, one ID per type, prepaid threshold), PIA, privacy notice, retention schedule, threat model | 1–2 weeks, mostly non-code | — | Signed PIA and counsel sign-off |
| P1 | Vault, save/prefill/revoke/delete, API + Blade + Flutter, audit on reveal, feature flag and kill switch | 5–8 dev-days | ₱15k–24k | Ownership isolation passes; order snapshot immutable |
| P2 | On-device capture, OCR prefill, confirmation UI, blur/glare gate, accuracy measurement | 8–12 dev-days | ₱24k–36k | Auto-fill acceptance ≥70%; no rise in abandonment; zero PII in logs |
| P3 | Exception review queue, duplicate and velocity rules, signed-URL viewer, appeals, metrics | 10–15 dev-days | ₱30k–45k | Review rate <5%; red-team false accept <0.5% |
| P4 | PhilSys eVerify or vendor eKYC | 20–30 dev-days plus contract and VAPT | ₱60k–90k plus fees | Measured loss exceeds total cost of control |

**Controls that must exist before the first ID is saved.** Access is the first layer: two-factor authentication on admin accounts, masked numbers everywhere except an explicit reveal that writes an audit row, and sellers limited to the snapshot for their own orders with no query access to the vault. Throughput is the second: roughly 5 credential creations and 20 OCR attempts per day per user, 10 reveals per hour, and 429s logged. Data handling is the third: images, if stored at all, in private object storage only, served through five-minute signed URLs with `no-store` and a watermark, deleted after **30 days (7 after review)**; credentials purged after 24 months of inactivity or on customer request, without touching order snapshots held under the tax obligation. Keys and incidents are the fourth: `APP_KEY` and the blind-index pepper in the platform secret store rather than the database backup, and a breach runbook that starts a **72-hour NPC clock** on discovery, with the full report due within five days ([RA 10173](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html); [NPC AO 2020-038](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Redacted-Advisory-Opinion-No.-2020-038.pdf)). Today the seller page prints the full number in the clear; either keep it visible and make the page non-cacheable, or move it behind a reveal action — but do not remove it, because the handover comparison is the actual control.

**What not to build.**

| Tempting build | Why not | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Face match, liveness, selfie verification | Verifies the buyer, not the beneficiary; biometrics are irrevocable SPI; NPC has halted biometric processing before; injection defeats liveness; fails elderly users | Handover binding, duplicate portrait and ID signals |
| Hologram, UV, OVI or microprint detection | Needs controlled multi-frame lighting or special optics; no LGU template exists | Accept that it cannot be checked remotely |
| Custom OCR model or self-hosted Tesseract pipeline | No Philippine training set; independent baseline is 54.5% field accuracy; labelling is the long pole | ML Kit plus user confirmation |
| Auto-approval from OCR confidence | Confidence measures legibility, not authenticity | Confidence routes to review, never to approval |
| Tokenization service or KMS envelope encryption | No Philippine ID tokenizer exists; adds a second system to secure | Encrypted cast plus HMAC pepper |
| Hard device binding | Locks out elderly users who change phones or share a family tablet | Device as a risk signal only |
| A public "verify ID" endpoint | Becomes an enumeration oracle for ID numbers | Authenticated, rate-limited, own-account only |
| Indefinite image retention | Breach surface with no BIR requirement behind it | Fields only; 30 days if an image is unavoidable |

**Open questions for counsel, and evidence gaps.** Ten questions block or shape the build: whether a for-profit pharmacy marketplace qualifies as a PhilSys relying party and what the real onboarding checklist and fees are (PSA eVerify); whether a PhilID alone satisfies RA 9994 §4's "other documents" catch-all for the senior medicine discount; the lawful basis for saving an ID number on an account — specific consent under DPA §13(a) versus "provided for by existing laws" under §13(b) — and whether that consent can be bundled with account creation under NPC Circular 2023-04; the BIR retention period for the sales record after the EOPT Act and whether any image must match it; the liability boundary for honouring a fake ID, and whether a verification log demonstrates good faith; NPC registration and DPIA obligations for processing SPI at scale under Circular 2023-06; whether NCDA permits automated queries against `verify.alaga.ph` and what it returns; whether the digital NSCID carries a verifiable QR that a third party may check; whether an upload satisfies the statutory requirement to "present" an ID; and whether a caregiver may hold another person's credential — the senior representative rule is explicit, the PWD equivalent is not.

The evidence gaps are equally material. There is **no public benchmark of OCR field accuracy on PhilID, OSCA, PWD, 4Ps, barangay or UMID cards**; no vendor publishes a template for any specific LGU design; no Philippine study measures what share of online discount claims are fraudulent or distinguishes counterfeit from borrowed from digitally manipulated; no data exists on smartphone and camera quality among Filipino seniors, which determines whether any capture flow is usable; and no published evaluation exists of UPWDIS accuracy, uptime or merchant adoption. Every threshold in the plan is therefore a local calibration, not a benchmark, and the pilot's specimen set — 30–50 cards per type across several LGUs, with worn, folded, laminated and glare variants, plus synthetic red-team samples — is the actual research instrument.

## Conclusion

The verification problem here is not a reading problem; it is a binding problem. Every technology that reads a card better — a larger document library, a stronger recogniser, a fine-tuned model — still returns text that a ₱40 print shop can produce, while the only signals that survive a determined counterfeiter are a government signature and a human who sees the card and the person together. That inverts the usual build order: the cheap, unglamorous controls (a saved credential with a revalidation date, duplicate detection, an in-app capture gate, a branch confirmation that feeds back into status) do the work, and the expensive ones answer questions nobody asked.

The regulatory direction is clear enough to plan against but not clear enough to depend on. UPWDIS will keep expanding toward 2029, NIDAS will keep onboarding relying parties, and each new QR-verifiable issuer makes the adapter layer more valuable and the OCR layer less so. Build the seam now, keep the manual path forever, and let coverage — not vendor demos — decide when a registry check becomes the default.
