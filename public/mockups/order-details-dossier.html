Order details modals — quiet dossier

Buyer · In progress · 760 px

Handoff notes

The same receipt, read in one glance

The redesign keeps JMMart’s quiet blue-and-navy ledger language but reorganises the dialog around the three questions people actually open it for: where is my order, what do I owe or need to do, and who has it. Hover or focus a note to highlight the part of the mockup it describes.

Notes are interactive — hover or tab to a card.

1

Status first, before the receipt

The first line answers what is happening and what comes next. Status is carried by an icon, a label, and a dot — never colour alone. The order reference is short, but the full ULID is one copy away.

2

A journey with honest dates

Four stages for buyers, five for sellers. Dates appear only where the data exists — created_at, shipped_at, delivered_at. A split order shows “1 of 2” as a half-filled node instead of pretending one parcel represents both stores.

3

Shipments, not a flat item list

Each store’s part keeps its own status, items, subtotal, and action. A buyer can confirm one parcel without touching the other. That is the real shape of a multi-store order, and it removes the “which items arrived?” question.

4

Money in one unmissable block

Subtotal, shipping, then the total as the largest number in the dialog. Payment state sits directly beneath it. Tabular numerals keep every amount aligned, and per-store subtotals reconcile to the order total above.

5

Read-only facts stay quiet

Delivery and payment are reference information, not actions. They share one calm two-column band with copy buttons only where copying is useful: the phone and the order number.

6

Seller: one next step, stated plainly

The seller modal leads with the single transition that moves the order forward. Guidance lives in the panel; the button lives in the footer. No competing toolbar, and no step that hides behind an icon.

7

Seller: facts in a rail

Items stay the main story. Payout, payment state, delivery address, and the buyer’s note sit in a narrower rail, so the seller can scan the order without the money disappearing below the fold.

8

Actions have rank

One blue primary action, quiet outline secondaries, and destructive actions behind More — separated from the button people press most. On a phone the primary action becomes a full-width 44px target above the safe-area inset.

Direction and tokens

brand ink muted sunken waiting done stopped
  • Widths — buyer 760px, seller 940px; full-height sheet under 720px.
  • Type — Inter throughout; 10–11px uppercase labels, 12–13px body, 20–24px totals. Tabular numerals for every amount.
  • Surfaces — white sheet, hairline dividers, sunken receipt band. Colour is reserved for state and the one primary action.
  • Motion — 220ms enter with an 8–10px lift and 0.986 scale; exit is shorter. All of it disappears under prefers-reduced-motion.

Build notes

  • The per-store Confirm received button maps to the existing orders.received endpoint on seller_orders; the modal already receives every seller order.
  • Step dates must come from real columns. Confirmed and processing have no timestamps today, so those labels stay dateless rather than inventing a timeline.
  • The seller More menu is where cancel, report failed delivery, and open full page belong; those actions keep their existing routes and confirmation dialogs.
  • Refund copy should render only from real payment state, not from order status alone. This mockup shows the happiest path.
  • Production keeps <dialog>, Escape and backdrop dismissal, focus return, and the existing fetch/popstate behaviour.

Mockup only — no Blade, route, or database file has been changed. Buyer and seller states are illustrative; amounts and names are fictional. The next step is to pick a direction, then port the shell and one state at a time.
